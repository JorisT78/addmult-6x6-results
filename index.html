Searching for a 6×6 additive-multiplicative magic square

Is there a 6×6 square of 36 distinct positive integers whose six rows, six columns and two diagonals all have the same sum S and the same product P? Such squares are known for larger orders, but none is known for order 6. This is a computer search for one.

No such square has been found so far. The search has found 3 272 near misses: squares in which all rows, all columns and the main diagonal have the same sum and the same product. Only the anti-diagonal fails.
In a few cases, only one of the 28 conditions in total (14 products, 14 sums) could not be met.

The closest squares

In each square, rows and columns are arranged so that the highlighted anti-diagonal is the one described. All 13 other lines have exactly the sum S and the product P.

27 of 28 conditions met

19 near misses have an arrangement whose anti-diagonal has the exact product P, 68 one whose anti-diagonal has the exact sum S, none both (both would be a full square). These are the results closest to a full square.

Chance of success

about 13 % per year

This is the estimated chance of finding a square within one year of uninterrupted searching on the two mini PCs, which search around the clock (the desktop PC and rented GPUs join only at times and are not counted). It is a rough model estimate, not a measurement: no square has ever been seen from which to calibrate it. The two unproved assumptions about how often a near miss can be completed give 6 % and 19 %; the figure above is their mixture.
